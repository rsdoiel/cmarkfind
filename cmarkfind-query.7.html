NAME

cmarkfind-query — the search syntax

DESCRIPTION

A query is a Lucene-style string, searched over each page’s title, description and body.

oberon compiler          both words (AND is implied)
oberon OR modula         either word
oberon NOT pascal        the first and not the second (-pascal is the same)
"shallow water"          the words next to each other, in order
compil*                  words that start with compil
title:oberon             the word in the title only (also description:, body:)
(oberon OR modula) -tide groups
author:doiel             pages whose author field has the word (see below)
keywords:"static sites"  all the words, in the field's values
datepublished:2026-06    a date field: the month (also 2026, 2026-06-15)
created:[2026-01 TO 2026-06]   from January 1 to June 30; { and } exclude a bound
created:[2026-01 TO 2026-07}   the same first half: } drops all of July
created:[2025 TO *]      * is no bound

A field of a page’s front matter can be searched when the page’s set maps it as fielded (cmarkfind-set(7); keywords and author are by default). The clause finds the pages whose field has every word you wrote, in any of its values; a phrase asks for all of its words and a trailing * makes the last a prefix. A clause on a field no set maps is read as words: note:text is the words note and text. A clause on a field one searched set maps and another does not matches nothing in the other, and is not an error. A clause on a field selects pages and does not rank them; the words of the text do. A date field (cmarkfind-set(7)) is queried with a date or a range of dates, in the forms YYYY, YYYY-MM and YYYY-MM-DD: a year or a month is the whole period, [ and ] include a bound, { and } exclude it, and an excluded year or month drops all of it ({2026-01 TO 2026-06} is February to May). A page with no date in the field is in no range. A range that cannot be read is read as words. Field names do not depend on case.

Case does not matter, nor does the spelling of an accent or a letter form: an accent written as a combining mark, a ligature, a full-width letter and the German sharp s match their plain forms (accents are not removed).

Chinese and Japanese are written without spaces, so each Han, Hiragana, Katakana or Bopomofo character is a word of its own and a query of several is the phrase of them: 東京都 finds the pages that have those three characters in a row, and that includes 東京都 inside a longer run of text. There is no dictionary; a search finds the characters you wrote, in order, and does not know where the words of a sentence begin and end. Korean is written with spaces and its words are indexed as written, with the particle attached (한국어를). A Korean word of two or more syllables in a query is therefore read as a prefix: 한국어 finds 한국어를, 한국어는 and 한국어. A word in quotation marks is not (the phrase “한국어” finds only the word 한국어), and a single syllable is not either. Thai, Lao, Khmer and Burmese are not segmented: a run of letters between spaces is one word, so a word in the middle of a clause cannot be found, only the start of it with a prefix (ภาษา*).

A title match counts for more than a description match, and a description match for more than one in the body (bm25 with weights 4, 2 and 1). Hits that rank the same are put in the order bookmarked, read, own, toc pages, then by address.

Text that is not valid query syntax (an unbalanced quote, a stray operator) is searched as plain words, so a typo returns hits and not an error; only a query over the size limit is refused (exit 2). Words of another search engine’s syntax that this one does not have, such as NEAR(a b), ^word, a “phrase”~3 or fuzzy word~, are read as the words they are made of. Pages marked gone are not searched. The text shown with a hit is the 14 words of the page that hold the most matches, each match in [brackets].

EXAMPLES

cmarkfind search '"shallow water" NOT tide'
cmarkfind search -- -dash
